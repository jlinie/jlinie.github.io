**Source:** [github.com/jlinie/LLM-textclassification](https://github.com/jlinie/LLM-textclassification)

## The Question

Everyone reaches for LLMs these days — but does a locally-run LLM actually
beat a well-tuned classical baseline on large-scale text classification? I
benchmarked both sides on 660K samples across 77 categories, comparing not
just accuracy but robustness, speed, and cost.

## How It Works

<TextWithImage image="/images/projects/p4-detail.jpg">
  The baseline: a TF-IDF vectorizer feeding a multinomial logistic regression,
  with a full precision/recall/F1 report per category. The challenger: Llama
  3.2 served locally via Ollama, using few-shot prompts enriched with entity
  annotations. A hybrid routing trick keeps things efficient — typical
  examples are handled cheaply, while only "atypical" texts (low TF-IDF
  confidence) get sent to the LLM, with API calls running in parallel and
  latency tracked throughout.
</TextWithImage>

The whole pipeline is reproducible with a Makefile (`make split`, `make
train`, `make eval`, `make llm`) and containerized with Docker.

## What I Learned

Training on a very large, imbalanced dataset with limited local compute
taught me more about practical ML than any textbook: LLM inference is slow
and costly at scale, classical baselines are shockingly hard to beat when
tuned well, and fair evaluation means looking far beyond a single accuracy
number.
