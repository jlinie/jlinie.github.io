**When:** Spring 2023, Machine Learning course (CS412) at Sabancı University

**Tech:** Python, Scikit-learn, Jupyter Notebook — KNN and Decision Tree classifiers

**Source:** [github.com/jlinie/CS412-Project](https://github.com/jlinie/CS412-Project)

## The Task

Given the classic German Credit dataset — applicant attributes like credit
history, employment, and loan purpose — predict whether extending credit is
a good or bad risk. A textbook problem, but one that mirrors what banks
genuinely deal with.

## What I Built

A complete machine learning pipeline: data cleaning and preprocessing,
feature engineering, then training and comparing K-Nearest Neighbors and
Decision Tree models, evaluated well beyond raw accuracy — because in credit
risk, a false "good risk" costs very differently than a false "bad risk."

## Looking Back

One of my earliest end-to-end ML projects, and the one where I first got
hooked on a question that still drives me: how much does data quality and
preprocessing matter compared to model choice? (Spoiler: a lot.)
