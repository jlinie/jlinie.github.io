**Status:** Active research interest

## The Question

Transformers conquered language, then vision — but *why* they work so well,
where attention genuinely earns its cost, and how the architecture should be
adapted outside NLP are still fascinatingly open questions. I want to
understand transformers at the mechanism level, not just as a black box to
fine-tune.

## How I'm Studying Them

My deep learning coursework at TU Darmstadt covers the architectural
foundations, and my forecasting project gives me a place to probe them
empirically: comparing transformer variants against recurrent and linear
models on the same task, where the differences (and the costs) become
concrete.

## Papers That Shaped My Thinking

- [*Attention Is All You Need*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)
  (Vaswani et al., 2017) — the original transformer paper: replacing
  recurrence entirely with self-attention. It taught me that architectural
  revolutions come from questioning assumptions everyone shares — nobody
  thought you could just delete recurrence from sequence modeling.
- [*An Image is Worth 16x16 Words*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)
  (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) — the Vision Transformer, treating image
  patches as tokens. It taught me how transferable the transformer recipe
  is once you find the right tokenization for a new domain — a lesson I now
  apply when thinking about time series as token sequences.
