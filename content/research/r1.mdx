**Status:** Active research interest — the thread connecting most of my work

## The Question

Modern AI progress often assumes unlimited clean data and unlimited compute.
Real problems rarely offer either. My core research interest is optimization
and efficiency in machine learning: how models can learn reliably under
constraints such as limited data, computational cost, and noisy or
incomplete datasets.

## Where It Comes From

<TextWithImage image="/images/research/r1-detail.jpg">
  This interest grew out of hands-on experience. Quantizing an RNN for blood
  sugar forecasting showed me how much efficiency matters in sensitive
  domains. Benchmarking a local LLM against logistic regression showed me how
  expensive "just use a bigger model" really is — and how competitive
  well-tuned small models remain. Both projects pointed to the same
  conclusion: the interesting frontier is doing more with less.
</TextWithImage>

## What I'm Doing About It

My coursework at TU Darmstadt — deep learning architectures and methods,
multimodal learning, statistical machine learning — is deliberately chosen
to build the theoretical foundations behind these questions: how
architectures can be optimized, and how statistical principles explain
learning behavior under uncertainty and limited data. My goal is to shape my
master's thesis around foundational ML questions with practical relevance,
ideally within a research lab here.
