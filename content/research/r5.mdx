**Status:** Active research interest — currently in the "reading everything" phase

## The Question

Diffusion models generate data by learning to reverse a gradual noising
process — and they've become the dominant approach in image generation. What
fascinates me is the elegance of the framing (generation as iterative
denoising) and the open frontier beyond images: diffusion for time series
forecasting, imputation, and data augmentation, which connects directly to
my other interests.

## Why Diffusion + Time Series?

Probabilistic forecasting needs more than point predictions — it needs
distributions over possible futures. Diffusion models are natural
distribution learners, and using them to sample plausible future
trajectories (or to generate synthetic training series for augmentation) is
an active, exciting research direction I want to explore hands-on.

## Papers That Shaped My Thinking

- [*Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)
  (Ho et al., 2020) — the DDPM paper that ignited the field: a simple
  denoising objective yielding stunning generative quality. It taught me
  that the "right" training objective can matter more than architectural
  cleverness — the U-Net wasn't new; the objective was.
- [*Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502)
  (Song et al., 2021) — is about accelerating sampling from hundreds of
  steps to a handful via non-Markovian processes. It taught me that
  diffusion's biggest practical weakness — slow inference — is an
  optimization problem, which ties beautifully into my interest in
  efficiency.
- [*CSDI: Conditional Score-based Diffusion Models for Probabilistic Time
  Series Imputation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03502) (Tashiro et al.,
  2021) — is about applying diffusion to fill gaps in multivariate time
  series. It taught me that diffusion is a general recipe, not an image
  trick — and it's the bridge between this interest and my forecasting work.
