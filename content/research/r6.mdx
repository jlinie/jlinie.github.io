## The Question

How does a neural network turn words into numbers that actually *mean*
something? word2vec showed that if you train a simple model to predict
words from their context, the resulting vectors organize themselves into a
space where semantic relationships become geometric ones — similar words
cluster together, and analogies become vector arithmetic.

I'm studying this now, and honestly, it's the closest thing to magic I've
seen in ML: nobody told the model that Paris relates to France the way Rome
relates to Italy. It discovered that structure purely from co-occurrence
statistics.

## Why It Matters Beyond Words

Embeddings are the quiet foundation of everything I care about: transformers
start with an embedding layer, multimodal models align embeddings across
data types, and even time series methods increasingly borrow the
"tokenize-then-embed" recipe. Understanding word2vec properly means
understanding the representational bedrock under modern deep learning.

## Papers That Shaped My Thinking

- [*Linguistic Regularities in Continuous Space Word Representations*](https://aclanthology.org/N13-1090/)
  (Mikolov et al., 2013) — is about the famous discovery that word vectors
  encode analogies as arithmetic: vector("king") − vector("man") +
  vector("woman") lands near vector("queen"). It taught me that
  representations learned for one objective (prediction) can capture
  structure nobody explicitly asked for — meaning itself can emerge as
  geometry.
- [*Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3781)
  (Mikolov et al., 2013) — the original word2vec paper introducing the
  Skip-gram and CBOW architectures. It taught me that radical simplification
  can be a superpower: by stripping away hidden layers, word2vec trained on
  billions of words and beat far more complex models — efficiency enabling
  scale, scale enabling quality.
